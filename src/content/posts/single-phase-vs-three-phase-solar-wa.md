For years, a common Perth answer to "can I install a bigger system?" was "only if you have three-phase power". That changed on 1 May 2026. New rules on the South West Interconnected System (SWIS), the grid that serves Perth and the south-west, now allow larger systems on standard connections, including a [13.2kW system](/residential-solar/13-2kw-solar-system/) on single phase. This guide explains what changed and what it means for your home.

## Single-phase vs three-phase: the basics

Most Perth homes have single-phase power: one active wire supplying the house. Some larger or newer homes, and many businesses, have three-phase power: three active wires, which spread heavy loads such as ducted air conditioning, workshops and EV chargers. You can usually tell by looking at the main switch in your switchboard: a three-phase main switch is three switches linked together.

## What changed on 1 May 2026

- **Bigger systems on a standard connection:** inverter systems up to an aggregate of 30 kVA can now connect under one standard connection, on single or three phase. The previous limit for most homes was 5 kW.
- **Inverter standard:** all new inverters must meet AS/NZS 4777.2, using Australian Region B settings, including the ability to ride through voltage disturbances without tripping.
- **Remote management:** new inverters must support remote management through the communications standard your retailer nominates. For Synergy customers this is CSIP-AUS. It allows Emergency Solar Management during rare low-demand events and enables flexible export products.
- **Fixed export option:** if your site cannot keep a stable internet connection, the system can instead be export-limited to 1.5 kW and does not need remote management.

The rules apply to systems installed or upgraded on or after 1 May 2026. Existing systems are not affected unless you change them.

## What this means if you are single phase

You are no longer locked out of a larger system just because you have single-phase power. A [10kW](/residential-solar/10kw-solar-system-wa/) or 13.2kW system may now be possible, subject to your retailer's export settings, your switchboard and your supply capacity. Your installer still has to design within what your connection and wiring can safely carry.

## When three phase still helps

- You run large loads at the same time, such as ducted air conditioning, an EV charger and a pool.
- You want a three-phase inverter to balance generation across phases.
- You plan a large battery with whole-home backup.

Upgrading to three phase involves Western Power and an electrician, and can be costly. It is rarely worth doing for solar alone now; ask for a design on your existing supply first.

## Emergency Solar Management: should you worry?

No. It is a last-resort measure for very low-demand, sunny periods when the grid has too much rooftop solar. It is expected to be used rarely and briefly. The WA Government notes that in South Australia, a similar tool was used once for about an hour in its first year.

## Why internet connection matters

Remote management needs your inverter online. If the connection drops, the system falls back to a lower export limit until it reconnects. Make sure your Wi-Fi reaches the inverter location, or ask about a wired or 4G option.

## Questions to ask your installer

1. Is the quoted inverter on the approved list and compliant with the May 2026 requirements?
2. Which export arrangement will my system be commissioned under?
3. What is my expected export limit?
4. Does my switchboard need work for a larger system?
5. Will my system be ready for a battery or VPP later?
