Solar systems need little maintenance, but "little" is not "none". Perth's long dry summers, sea salt near the coast and dust from building sites all collect on panels. A simple schedule keeps your system safe and producing as it should. It's part of what we cover in [solar panel installation in WA](/solar-packages/solar-panel-installation-in-wa/).

## Why Perth panels get dirty

Perth can go months in summer with almost no rain. Dust, pollen, bird droppings and smoke particles build up, and light rain often just moves dirt to the bottom edge of the panel. Flat-mounted panels on commercial roofs hold dirt even more.

## How often to clean

| Situation | Suggested cleaning |
| --- | --- |
| Typical suburban home, pitched roof | Once a year, ideally late spring before summer |
| Near the coast (salt) or busy roads | Twice a year |
| Near building sites, farms or industrial areas | Every 3–6 months, or when monitoring shows a drop |
| Low-tilt or flat commercial arrays | Every 6 months |

## How to clean safely

The safest option is to hire a professional with working-at-height training. If panels are reachable from the ground:

- Use a soft brush or sponge with clean, preferably deionised, water.
- Avoid detergents, abrasive pads and high-pressure washers, which can damage coatings and seals.
- Clean early morning or late afternoon, when panels are cool.
- Never walk on panels or climb onto a roof without proper safety equipment.

Check your panel warranty for approved cleaning methods.

## What a professional inspection checks

1. Panels for cracks, hot spots, delamination and discolouration.
2. Mounting rails, clamps and roof penetrations for corrosion and leaks.
3. DC isolators and cabling for water entry, UV damage and loose connections.
4. Inverter error logs, ventilation and firmware.
5. Switchboard protection, labels and the earth connection.
6. Generation compared with expected output for the season.
7. Battery health, if installed.

We recommend an inspection every 2–3 years for homes and every year for commercial systems.

## Warning signs to act on

- A sudden drop in output of 20% or more on sunny days.
- Error codes or red lights on the inverter.
- The monitoring app shows zero generation during daylight.
- Burning smell, buzzing or scorch marks near the inverter or isolators.
- Water inside an isolator cover.

If you see scorch marks, smell burning or see water in electrical equipment, switch off using the shutdown procedure on your system label and call a licensed electrician. Do not touch damaged components.

## Keep monitoring connected

Since the May 2026 connection rules, many new systems rely on an internet connection for remote management. If your Wi-Fi changes, reconnect the inverter. Without it, exports may be limited until it is back online.
