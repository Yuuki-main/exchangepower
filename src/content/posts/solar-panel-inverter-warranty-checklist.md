A solar system should run for 20 years or more. The equipment you choose and the warranties behind it decide how smooth those years are. Use this checklist to compare quotes on substance, not sales language, whether you're looking at our [solar packages](/solar-packages/) or anyone else's.

## Panels: what the specs mean

| Spec | What it tells you | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power (W) | Output of one panel in test conditions | 400–480 W is common for homes in 2026 |
| Efficiency (%) | Power per square metre | Higher helps on small roofs |
| Temperature coefficient (%/°C) | Output lost per degree of heat | Closer to zero is better in Perth summers |
| Degradation | How fast output declines each year | A low first-year loss and low annual loss |
| Cell type | N-type, TOPCon, heterojunction, PERC | N-type designs generally degrade more slowly |

Ask for the exact model number and check it is on the Clean Energy Council approved products list. Only listed products are eligible for STCs.

## What "Tier 1" really means

"Tier 1" is a financial ranking of manufacturers based on bank-financed projects. It says a maker is large and bankable. It does not test panel quality. It is a useful filter, not a guarantee.

## Inverters: the part that works hardest

The inverter converts solar DC power to AC for your home. It runs every day and is the component most likely to need service.

- Check it meets AS/NZS 4777.2 and the WA connection requirements in force since 1 May 2026, including remote management.
- Choose string, hybrid or microinverters to suit your roof and battery plans (see [what changes the price of solar](/solar-system-cost-perth/)).
- Look for a local Australian service team and clear warranty claim process.
- Make sure monitoring works on your phone and can be shared with your installer.

## Four warranties to read

| Warranty | Typical length | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Panel product | 12–25 years | Defects in materials and manufacturing |
| Panel performance | 25–30 years | Minimum output after a set number of years, often around 85–90% |
| Inverter | 5–10 years, extendable | Inverter failure; check if labour and freight are included |
| Workmanship | Set by installer (ours is 5 years) | Installation faults: wiring, mounting, roof penetrations, leaks |

Batteries usually have a separate warranty of around 10 years with conditions on retained capacity or energy throughput. Read the fine print.

## Who stands behind the warranty?

A long warranty is only as good as the company honouring it. Check:

1. Does the manufacturer have an Australian office and phone number?
2. Who handles the claim: you, the installer or the manufacturer?
3. Are removal and reinstallation labour costs covered?
4. Is the installer a local WA business that will still be around?

Australian Consumer Law also gives you guarantees that products are of acceptable quality, separate from any written warranty.

## Installation details that protect you

- SAA-accredited designer and installer.
- Licensed electrical contractor for all electrical work.
- Correct rooftop isolators and labelling.
- Roof penetrations sealed and tiles replaced if broken.
- A handover pack: compliance certificate, single-line diagram, warranties, monitoring login.
