Two Perth solar quotes for the "same" 6.6kW system can be thousands of dollars apart. That gap is rarely random. It comes from a handful of choices about equipment, your roof and your switchboard. This guide explains each one, so you can compare quotes on what you actually get. For the sizes we install, see our [residential solar systems](/residential-solar/).

## The short answer

The price of a solar system in Perth depends mainly on its size, the panel and inverter brands, the type of roof, and any electrical upgrades your home needs. The federal Small-scale Technology Certificate (STC) discount is taken off the price upfront, and it gets smaller every 1 January until 2030.

## 1. System size

Bigger systems cost more in total but less per kilowatt, because the fixed costs of design, travel, approvals and labour are spread over more panels. The right size comes from your bills, not the roof alone. Look at your daily usage in kWh and when you use it. A household that runs air conditioning in the afternoon can use far more solar directly than one that is empty all day.

## 2. Panel quality and brand

Panels differ in efficiency, degradation rate, product warranty and the strength of the company behind that warranty. Higher-efficiency panels make more power from the same roof area, which matters on small or shaded roofs. Ask for the exact model number on your quote, not just the brand, and check it on the Clean Energy Council approved-products list.

## 3. Inverter type

The inverter is the part most likely to need attention in the system's life, so it deserves as much thought as the panels.

- **String inverter:** one central unit; the lowest cost per kW and suits simple, unshaded roofs.
- **Hybrid inverter:** manages panels and a battery together; costs more now but makes adding a battery later simpler.
- **Microinverters or optimisers:** electronics on each panel; better on roofs with shade or several directions, with panel-level monitoring.

Since 1 May 2026, every new inverter on Perth's main grid must also meet the new connection rules, including remote management capability. A compliant quote should say so.

## 4. Your roof

Tin (Colorbond) roofs are usually the quickest to install on. Terracotta and concrete tile roofs take longer and carry a higher risk of breakage. Two-storey homes, steep pitches and split arrays across several roof faces all add labour, scaffolding or extra cabling.

## 5. Switchboard and electrical work

Older homes may need a switchboard upgrade, a new main switch, or extra space for solar isolators. Some homes also need a meter change through Synergy. A good installer checks this before quoting, rather than adding it on installation day.

## 6. The STC discount

The STC discount reduces the upfront price of every eligible solar system up to 100kW. Perth is in Zone 3. The number of certificates depends on system size and the year of installation. For a 6.6kW system installed in 2026, that is about 45 STCs (6.6 × 1.382 × 5 years). At a certificate price of around $35–$40, that is roughly $1,600–$1,800 off. From 1 January 2027 the same system earns about 36 STCs. Read [Solar Rebate Drops on 1 January 2027](/solar-rebate-drop-january-2027-wa/) for the full comparison, or see every incentive in [solar rebates and incentives in WA](/solar-packages/solar-rebates-and-incentives-in-wa/).

## A simple payback example

This example is illustrative only. Your figures depend on your tariff, usage pattern and roof.

- 6.6kW system in Perth producing about 26 kWh a day on average.
- 40% used in the home (10.4 kWh) at about 32c/kWh on the Synergy Home Plan (A1): about $3.33 a day.
- 60% exported (15.6 kWh), mostly before 3pm, at the DEBS off-peak rate of 2c/kWh: about $0.31 a day.
- Total value: around $3.60 a day, or about $1,330 a year.

Divide the installed price by your yearly saving to estimate payback. The more solar you use at home, the faster the payback, because every kWh you use saves you the full tariff rather than earning 2c.

## Red flags in a cheap quote

- No panel or inverter model numbers.
- No mention of the May 2026 connection rules or remote management.
- "Free" switchboard work that is not written into the quote.
- No written workmanship warranty or no WA business address.
- Pressure to sign the same day.

## How to compare quotes properly

Put each quote in a simple table: system size, panel model, inverter model, warranty years (product and workmanship), what electrical work is included, monitoring, and the final price after STCs. Then compare price per kW and what is excluded. The cheapest line is often not the cheapest system over 15 years.
