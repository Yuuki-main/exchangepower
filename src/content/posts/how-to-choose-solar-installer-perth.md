The installer you choose matters as much as the equipment. A good installer designs the system for your home, installs it safely, handles the paperwork and is still around when you need support. Use these ten checks before you sign. You can check us against them too: read [about Exchange Power](/about-exchange-power/).

## 1. SAA accreditation

The designer and installer should hold current accreditation with Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA). Battery installations need Grid Connected Battery System accreditation. Check the accreditation number on SAA's online status check. Only accredited installations are eligible for STCs and the federal battery discount.

## 2. WA electrical contractor licence

All electrical work in WA must be done by a licensed electrical contractor. Ask for the EC licence number and check it with Building and Energy WA.

## 3. NETCC Approved Seller

The New Energy Tech Consumer Code (NETCC) sets standards for how solar and battery businesses sell, contract and support customers. Approved Sellers have committed to the Code. Check the business on the NETCC website.

## 4. A local WA business with a real address

Look for a WA street address, an ABN and a local phone number. A local team is easier to reach for warranty and service calls.

## 5. A written, itemised quote

The quote should list panel and inverter (and battery) models, quantities, system size, STC discount, electrical work included, monitoring, exclusions and the final price.

## 6. Approved products only

Panels, inverters and batteries should be on the Clean Energy Council approved products list. For the WA battery rebate, the battery must also be on the Synergy or Horizon Power supported list.

## 7. Compliance with the 2026 WA rules

Since 1 May 2026, new systems on Perth's main grid must meet updated requirements, including remote management or a fixed 1.5 kW export setting. Ask how your system will be commissioned.

## 8. Clear warranties in writing

You should receive the panel, inverter and battery warranty terms and the installer's own workmanship warranty, with who to call for each. See our [panels, inverters and warranties checklist](/solar-panel-inverter-warranty-checklist/).

## 9. Rebate handling

A good installer applies the STC discount on the quote and, for batteries, is accredited to lodge the WA Residential Battery Scheme application through Plenti. See the [no-interest loan guide](/wa-battery-scheme-no-interest-loan/).

## 10. No pressure, real reviews

Avoid same-day-only prices and aggressive door-to-door selling. If you agree to buy from an unsolicited approach, Australian Consumer Law gives you a cooling-off period. Read recent reviews on independent platforms, and ask for local references.

## Red flags at a glance

- No model numbers on the quote.
- A price far below every other quote.
- No WA address or licence number.
- Promises of exact bill savings with no usage data.
- Requests for full payment upfront before installation.
