Many people are surprised to learn that rooftop solar stops working in a blackout. A battery can change that, but only if the system is designed for backup. Here is what to expect and what to ask for. For an overview of [home solar batteries in Perth](/solar-battery/), start there.

## Why solar switches off when the grid goes down

Grid-connected inverters must stop exporting when the grid fails. This protects line workers who may be repairing the network. So on a sunny day during an outage, panels alone will not power your home.

## A battery does not automatically mean backup

Some batteries are installed for bill savings only. Backup needs three things:

1. An inverter or gateway that can disconnect your home from the grid and form its own small grid.
2. Wiring that separates the circuits to be backed up.
3. Settings that keep a reserve in the battery for outages.

If backup matters to you, say so at the quote stage. It changes the hardware and the switchboard work.

## Three levels of backup

| Option | What stays on | Typical needs | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No backup | Nothing during an outage | Lowest cost | Savings-only buyers |
| Essential circuits | Lights, fridge, internet, selected power points | Backup sub-board, moderate inverter size | Most homes |
| Whole home | Everything, within inverter limits | Larger inverter or gateway, often three phase for large loads | Homes with medical needs, home businesses, frequent outages |

## What a battery can run, and for how long

Two numbers decide it: power (kW) and stored energy (kWh). A battery with 5 kW continuous power can run lights, a fridge, the internet, a TV and a microwave at once, but probably not ducted air conditioning as well. As a rough guide, a 10 kWh battery could keep a fridge, lights, internet and some power points going for 12–24 hours.

| Appliance | Approx. power | Approx. energy per day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fridge-freezer | 0.1–0.3 kW while running | 1–1.5 kWh |
| LED lights (whole home) | 0.1–0.3 kW | 0.5–1 kWh |
| Wi-Fi and modem | 0.02 kW | 0.5 kWh |
| TV | 0.1–0.2 kW | 0.5–1 kWh |
| Split air conditioner | 1–2.5 kW | 5–15 kWh |
| Electric oven | 2–3.5 kW | 1–2 kWh per use |

## Can solar recharge the battery during an outage?

With the right hybrid inverter and backup design, yes: panels can keep charging the battery during daylight, extending backup for days. With some AC-coupled designs, solar stays off during an outage. Ask specifically whether your design allows solar charging in backup mode. See [AC vs DC coupling](/add-battery-to-existing-solar-perth/).

## Changeover time

Some systems switch to backup in a fraction of a second, so computers stay on. Others take a few seconds, so clocks reset and devices reboot. Check the spec if you run sensitive equipment.

## VPPs and backup

In WA, batteries claiming the state rebate must join a VPP. Most VPP programs keep a reserve for your home. Check the reserve level so you are not left with an empty battery during an outage after a VPP event. Read [Virtual Power Plants in WA](/virtual-power-plant-wa/).

## Safety first

- Never connect a portable generator into your switchboard without a proper changeover switch installed by a licensed electrician.
- Backup wiring must be designed and installed to Australian Standards.
- Label backed-up circuits so everyone at home knows what will work.
