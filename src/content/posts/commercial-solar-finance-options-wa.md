Commercial solar can pay for itself, but many businesses prefer not to tie up cash. There are four common ways to fund a [commercial solar](/commercial-solar/) system. Each changes who owns it, who carries the risk and how quickly you see savings.

## The four options at a glance

| Option | Who owns the system | Upfront cost | Cash flow | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash purchase | You | Full price | Largest long-term saving | Businesses with spare capital |
| Equipment finance or loan | You (lender may hold security) | Low or none | Repayments often below savings | Owners wanting ownership without cash outlay |
| Lease (operating or finance) | Financier, then possibly you | None | Fixed lease payments | Businesses wanting predictable costs |
| Power purchase agreement (PPA) | Provider | None | Pay per kWh used, below grid rate | Large sites, multi-site groups |

## 1. Cash purchase

You pay upfront and keep 100% of the savings. Payback is usually fastest in absolute terms, and the system becomes a business asset that may be depreciable. The trade-off is the opportunity cost of that cash.

## 2. Equipment finance or business loan

A lender funds the system and you repay over a set term, often 3–7 years. With a well-sized system, monthly savings can match or exceed repayments, so the project can be close to cash-flow neutral from day one. You own the system, which may affect how it is treated for tax. Compare interest rates, fees, early-payout terms and balloon payments.

## 3. Leasing

A financier owns the system and leases it to you for a fixed monthly payment. At the end of the term you may be able to buy it, extend the lease or return it. Leasing keeps capital free and costs predictable, but total cost over the term is usually higher than buying.

## 4. Power purchase agreement

A provider installs and owns the system on your roof. You pay only for the solar power you use, at a rate below your grid tariff, often for 10–20 years. There is no upfront cost and the provider handles maintenance. PPAs suit larger sites and usually need a long lease or ownership of the building.

## Questions to ask any financier

1. What is the total cost over the term, including fees?
2. Who is responsible for maintenance, insurance and inverter replacement?
3. What happens if we move, sell the building or close?
4. Are there performance guarantees on generation?
5. Can we pay out early, and at what cost?

## Do not forget the STC discount

Systems up to 100kW are eligible for STCs, which reduce the price before financing. Make sure the discount is applied to the amount financed, not kept by a third party without a lower price.

## Tax treatment

How each option is treated for tax depends on ownership and structure. Interest, lease payments and depreciation can be handled differently. Speak with your accountant before choosing.
