Commercial solar sizes are not interchangeable. The right size depends on how much power your business uses during the day, how much roof you have, and how the network connection will be handled. Here is a side-by-side comparison of the three most common sizes for WA businesses, from our [commercial solar](/commercial-solar/) range.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Feature | 30kW | 50kW | 100kW |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Panels (about 440 W) | About 68 | About 114 | About 227 |
| Roof area (approx.) | 150–170 m² | 250–280 m² | 500–560 m² |
| Average daily output in Perth* | About 130 kWh | About 220 kWh | About 440 kWh |
| Annual output* | About 48,000 kWh | About 80,000 kWh | About 160,000 kWh |
| STCs if installed in 2026 | About 207 | About 345 | About 691 |
| Network connection | Can fit within the 30 kVA standard connection | Western Power application required | Western Power application required |
| Typical business | Offices, clinics, small workshops, retail | Larger workshops, gyms, supermarkets, schools | Warehouses, cold storage, manufacturing, large retail |

*Estimates using about 4.4 kWh per kW per day. Site design gives exact figures.

## How to choose

Size to your daytime load first. As a rule of thumb, a system whose output is used mostly on site gives the best return. Look at your weekday usage between 9am and 3pm. If it averages 15 kW across those hours, a system around 30kW will mostly be self-consumed. If it averages 40–50 kW, look at 100kW.

## 30kW: the efficient starting point

A [30kW system](/commercial-solar/30kw-solar-system/) suits businesses using roughly 150–300 kWh on a typical weekday. It can usually connect under the standard connection rules introduced on 1 May 2026, which simplifies approval. It is a strong choice for offices, medical centres, small workshops and retail.

## 50kW: the mid-size workhorse

A [50kW system](/commercial-solar/50kw-solar-system/) suits businesses with steady daytime loads such as refrigeration, compressors or air conditioning across a larger floor area. It needs a Western Power application and assessment, which may set export limits. Plan for that in your timeline.

## 100kW: maximum STC-eligible size

A [100kW system](/commercial-solar/100kw-solar-system/) is the largest size eligible for STCs. Above 100kW, systems move to a different certificate scheme with different paperwork. It suits sites with large roofs and heavy daytime use, such as warehouses, cold stores and manufacturers. Export limits are common, so self-consumption is critical.

## Things that change the right size

- **Weekend operation:** if you close on weekends, two days of output export at a low rate.
- **Roof structure:** older roofs may need an engineer's check for added load.
- **Switchboard capacity:** larger systems may need main switchboard upgrades.
- **Future loads:** EV fleet charging or electrified processes may justify a bigger system now.
- **Battery:** a commercial battery can shift surplus into late-afternoon peaks and help with demand.

## Staging a project

Some businesses start with 30kW, then add more later. This can work, but each upgrade must meet the connection rules at the time, and it is usually cheaper per kW to install the final size at once.
