A [10kW solar system](/residential-solar/10kw-solar-system-wa/) in Perth produces about 40–44 kWh a day on average across the year, or roughly 14,500–16,000 kWh a year. That is an estimate. Your actual output depends on roof direction, tilt, shade, heat, inverter size and export settings. This guide shows what to expect in each season and how to check your system.

## Average output by season

Perth is one of Australia's sunniest capitals, averaging around 4.4 kWh of generation per kW of panels each day. Output swings with day length and sun angle.

| Season | Typical daily output (10kW, north-facing) | What drives it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summer (Dec–Feb) | About 55–62 kWh | Long days, high sun; heat trims panel efficiency slightly |
| Autumn (Mar–May) | About 35–45 kWh | Shorter days, lower sun |
| Winter (Jun–Aug) | About 25–32 kWh | Short days, cloud and rain fronts |
| Spring (Sep–Nov) | About 45–55 kWh | Cool, clear days are very productive |

These are guide figures. Your installer's design report should give month-by-month estimates for your exact roof.

## Why your system may produce less

- **Orientation:** east or west-facing panels produce roughly 10–15% less across the day than north-facing, but more in the morning or late afternoon. See [how roof direction and shading affect solar](/roof-orientation-shading-solar-perth/).
- **Shade:** a tree, chimney or neighbouring wall can cut output from a whole string of panels on a string inverter.
- **Heat:** panels lose a little efficiency as they get hotter. Perth summer roofs can exceed 60°C.
- **Dust and bird droppings:** Perth's dry summers leave dust that rain does not wash off until winter. See our [cleaning and maintenance schedule](/solar-panel-cleaning-maintenance-perth/).
- **Inverter clipping:** if panels are oversized relative to the inverter, peak output is capped on bright middays. A small amount is normal and planned.
- **Export limits:** if your site cannot keep remote management connected, exports may be limited to 1.5 kW, which can reduce how much you generate when the house is not using power.

## What 10kW of solar can run

At 40–44 kWh a day, a 10kW system can typically cover daytime running of ducted air conditioning, a pool pump on a timer, a dishwasher and washing machine, a home office, and daytime EV charging, with surplus to charge a battery in most months.

## Turn output into savings

Output only becomes savings when you use it or store it. Move flexible loads into the solar window:

1. Run the pool pump between 10am and 3pm.
2. Set the dishwasher and washing machine on a delay timer for late morning.
3. Pre-cool the house in the early afternoon before the 3pm peak.
4. Put a heat-pump hot water system on a midday timer.
5. Charge the EV in the middle of the day.

If you are on Synergy's Midday Saver plan, daytime grid power is also cheaper, which changes the maths. Check your tariff before choosing.

## How to check your system is performing

Open your inverter app and look at the past 30 days. Compare daily output with the seasonal table above, adjusted for your roof. Red flags include a sudden drop of 20% or more on sunny days, one string reading zero, or error codes. A professional inspection can check panels, connections and the inverter safely.
