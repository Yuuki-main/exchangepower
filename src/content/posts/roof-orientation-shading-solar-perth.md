Perth sits at about 32° south, so the sun travels across the northern sky. That makes a north-facing roof the best single direction for solar. But many Perth homes have east, west or split roofs, and they can still deliver excellent savings when the system is designed for them. Good [solar panel installation in WA](/solar-packages/solar-panel-installation-in-wa/) starts with your roof.

## How direction changes output

| Panel direction | Approx. yearly output vs north | When it produces most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| North | 100% | Midday, evenly through the day |
| North-west / north-east | About 95–98% | Slightly later / earlier |
| West | About 85–90% | Afternoon and into the 3pm–9pm peak |
| East | About 85–90% | Morning |
| South | About 65–75% | Low; generally avoided |

These are typical estimates for Perth roof pitches. Your design report should model your exact roof.

## Why west can beat north for some homes

Synergy pays 10c/kWh for exports from 3pm to 9pm, but only 2c before 3pm (see [DEBS explained](/debs-synergy-solar-feed-in-explained/)). Many households also run air conditioning in the late afternoon. West-facing panels produce less in total but more of it when you need it. A split north-west design often gives the best balance of total output and late-afternoon generation.

## East-west split systems

Putting panels on both east and west roofs spreads generation across a longer part of the day. Peak output is lower, which suits a smaller inverter and reduces clipping. You need either an inverter with two independent MPPT inputs or panel-level electronics, so each roof face is managed separately.

## Tilt angle

The ideal fixed tilt for yearly output in Perth is around 25–32°. Most Perth pitched roofs are 20–25°, which loses very little. Flat roofs on carports, patios and commercial buildings need tilt frames, typically 10–15°, to balance output, wind load and self-cleaning in rain.

## Shading: the biggest hidden loss

Panels in a string share current. If one panel is shaded by a tree, chimney, aerial or the neighbour's second storey, the whole string can drop. Ways to manage it:

- **Design around it:** keep panels off shaded areas, even if it means fewer panels.
- **Separate strings:** put shaded and unshaded panels on different MPPT inputs.
- **Optimisers or microinverters:** electronics on each panel limit losses to that panel and give panel-level monitoring.
- **Trim trees:** often the cheapest fix, with council and neighbour approval.

Ask your installer for a shade analysis across both summer and winter. The winter sun is lower, so shadows are longer.

## Roof type and condition

Colorbond, Zincalume, terracotta and concrete tile roofs all take solar. Check that the roof has at least 10–15 years of life left, because removing and refitting panels later adds cost. Asbestos roofs need specialist advice and usually replacement before installation.

## What a good design report shows

1. A roof layout with each panel placed.
2. Expected output per month.
3. Shade analysis for summer and winter.
4. Inverter loading and any planned clipping.
5. Export setting under the 2026 connection rules.
