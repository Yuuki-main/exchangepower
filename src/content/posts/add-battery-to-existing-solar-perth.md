Most Perth homes with solar can add a battery without replacing their panels. The main decision is how the battery connects: AC coupling or DC coupling. Each has trade-offs in cost, efficiency and future flexibility. For an overview, see [home solar batteries in Perth](/solar-battery/).

## AC coupling: the battery has its own inverter

An AC-coupled battery connects to your home's AC wiring, next to your existing solar inverter. Your solar keeps working as it does today. Surplus solar flows into the battery through the battery's own inverter.

**Good for:** systems with a healthy, fairly new string inverter; homes that want minimal change to the existing setup.

**Trade-offs:** energy is converted DC to AC to DC to AC, so a few per cent more is lost. You maintain two inverters.

## DC coupling: one hybrid inverter runs both

A DC-coupled system uses a hybrid inverter that manages the panels and the battery together. Usually your existing string inverter is replaced with a hybrid.

**Good for:** older inverters nearing end of life; systems being upgraded with more panels at the same time.

**Trade-offs:** higher upfront cost if your current inverter still works; the hybrid must be compatible with your panels' voltage and current.

| Factor | AC-coupled | DC-coupled (hybrid) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing inverter | Kept | Usually replaced |
| Efficiency | Slightly lower | Slightly higher |
| Upfront cost | Often lower if inverter is newer | Higher, but resets inverter age |
| Upgrading panels later | Flexible | Limited by hybrid inverter size |
| Best for | Inverters under about 6–7 years old | Older inverters or a full upgrade |

## Check your existing system first

1. **Inverter age and brand:** if it is 8+ years old or out of warranty, replacing it with a hybrid often makes sense.
2. **System size:** a small, older system (for example 3kW) may not produce enough surplus to fill a battery in winter. Adding panels may help.
3. **Condition:** a safety inspection of isolators, cabling and panels before adding a battery.
4. **Switchboard:** space for new protection devices and, if wanted, a backup circuit sub-board.

## Rules that apply when you add a battery

- **Connection rules:** since 1 May 2026, new or upgraded components must meet the current WA requirements, including AS/NZS 4777.2 and remote management or a fixed 1.5 kW export option. See [single-phase vs three-phase solar in WA](/single-phase-vs-three-phase-solar-wa/).
- **WA rebate:** the battery must be on the Synergy or Horizon Power supported list, installed by an SAA Grid Connected Battery System accredited installer, and enrolled in an approved VPP.
- **Buyback scheme:** if you are on the older REBS buyback, adding a battery usually moves you to DEBS. Ask Synergy how your account will change before you sign. See [DEBS explained](/debs-synergy-solar-feed-in-explained/).

## What it costs and what you save

Costs depend on battery size, coupling method and switchboard work. Savings come from replacing evening grid power with stored solar. The WA state rebate (up to $1,300 Synergy or $3,800 Horizon Power) and the federal discount reduce the upfront cost; see [WA residential battery rebates](/solar-battery/wa-residential-battery-rebates/). [Is a solar battery worth it in Perth](/is-solar-battery-worth-it-perth/) gives a payback method.

## Typical installation day

Most retrofits take one day. The installer mounts the battery, fits protection and any backup sub-board, commissions the inverter settings, connects monitoring, and completes VPP enrolment steps.
