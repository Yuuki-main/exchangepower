If you want the WA Government battery rebate, your battery must join an approved Virtual Power Plant (VPP). For many Perth homeowners that is the most confusing part of buying a battery. Here is what a VPP is, what it does with your battery, and how to choose one. For the rebate itself, see [WA residential battery rebates](/solar-battery/wa-residential-battery-rebates/).

## What a VPP is

A VPP is a network of home batteries that a retailer or aggregator can coordinate. On a hot evening when demand peaks, the VPP can ask enrolled batteries to discharge a little power to the grid at the same time. Together, thousands of small batteries act like one power station.

## Why WA requires it

Perth's main grid is isolated from the rest of Australia, so it cannot import power from other states at peak times. Coordinated home batteries help keep the grid stable and reduce the need for expensive peak generation. That public benefit is why the WA rebate is linked to VPP participation.

## The rules in brief

- To receive the WA state rebate or no-interest loan, the battery must be enrolled in an approved VPP.
- The battery and inverter must be on the Synergy or Horizon Power supported-solution list.
- The installer must hold Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA) Grid Connected Battery System accreditation.
- The federal Cheaper Home Batteries discount does not require you to join a VPP, though the battery must be VPP-capable.

## VPP options in WA

| Your retailer | VPP options | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synergy (Perth and south-west) | Synergy Battery Rewards; other approved third-party VPPs such as Plico | Check current payments and terms on Synergy's site |
| Horizon Power (regional WA) | Community Wave | Members may access Horizon Power's Buyback Bonus |

Approved options can change. Your installer should confirm which VPPs your battery model supports.

## What the VPP does with your battery

In normal operation your battery charges from your solar and powers your home in the evening as usual. During a limited number of grid events, the VPP may discharge some of your stored energy, usually in the late afternoon or evening. Most programs keep a reserve for your home and limit how often events happen. Read each program's rules on event frequency, reserve level and backup.

## What you earn

VPP benefits vary. They may include sign-up credits, ongoing bill credits, or payments for energy exported during events. Exports during 3pm–9pm already earn the DEBS peak rate of 10c/kWh for Synergy customers (see [DEBS explained](/debs-synergy-solar-feed-in-explained/)). Compare programs on:

1. Upfront or sign-up credit.
2. Ongoing payments per event or per kWh.
3. Minimum enrolment period and exit conditions.
4. Reserve kept for your home and blackout backup.
5. Any tariff you must be on.

Program payments change, so check each one's current terms on the retailer's site before you enrol.

## Common worries, answered

- **Will the VPP empty my battery?** Programs typically draw only part of the stored energy and keep a reserve.
- **Will it shorten battery life?** Events add some cycling, but within the warranty's normal use. Check your battery warranty terms.
- **Can I leave?** Most programs allow exit, but leaving early may affect rebate conditions. Read the scheme terms before you enrol.

## Is a VPP worth it?

For most WA households, yes, because the VPP unlocks the state rebate and loan. The extra income is usually modest; the rebate is the main financial benefit.
