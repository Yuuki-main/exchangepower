If your home runs ducted air conditioning, a pool pump, a home office or an electric vehicle, a standard 6.6kW system will not cover much of your bill. The real choice for many larger Perth homes is between the [10kW system](/residential-solar/10kw-solar-system-wa/) and the [13.2kW system](/residential-solar/13-2kw-solar-system/). Here is how they compare and how to decide.

## The short answer

Choose 10kW if your usage is around 25–40 kWh a day and you have about 50 m² of good roof. Choose 13.2kW if you use more than 40 kWh a day, plan an EV or battery soon, and have about 60 m² of north, east or west-facing roof.

| Feature | 10kW system | 13.2kW system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Panels (about 440 W each) | 23 | 30 |
| Roof area needed | About 46–50 m² | About 60–65 m² |
| Average daily output in Perth* | About 40–44 kWh | About 52–58 kWh |
| Best for | 4–5 people, pool, home office | 5+ people, EV, battery, all-electric home |
| STCs if installed in 2026 | About 69 | About 91 |
| Phase | Single or three phase | Often three phase; confirm with installer |

*Estimate using about 4.4 kWh per kW per day. Actual output depends on orientation, shade, inverter size and export settings.

## Start with your usage, not your roof

Your Synergy bill shows average daily usage in kWh. The more of that usage falls between 9am and 3pm, the more value you get from extra panels. Exports earn only 2c/kWh before 3pm under DEBS, while every kWh you use yourself saves around 32c on the Home Plan (A1). So extra panels pay off only if you can use their output or store it.

## When 10kW is the smarter choice

- Your daily use is 25–40 kWh and most of it is afternoon cooling.
- Your roof has limited space or some shading.
- You want a strong saving without paying for capacity you may not use.
- You may add a battery later, and 10kW leaves enough surplus to charge one in most seasons.

## When 13.2kW is worth the extra cost

- You charge an EV at home during the day, or plan to.
- You are electrifying: heat-pump hot water, induction cooking, replacing gas heating.
- You want a 13 kWh or larger battery to fill up even in winter.
- You have the roof space and the price difference per kW is small.

In winter, Perth panels produce roughly half their summer output. A 13.2kW system gives more headroom on short days, which matters most if you rely on a battery overnight.

## Phase, inverters and the 2026 connection rules

From 1 May 2026, homes on Perth's main grid can connect inverter systems up to 30 kVA on a standard connection, on single or three phase. Your retailer still sets export settings, and inverters must support remote management or be export-limited to 1.5 kW. Many 13.2kW designs use a three-phase inverter to spread load across phases. [Single-phase vs three-phase solar in WA](/single-phase-vs-three-phase-solar-wa/) explains what this means for your home.

## Cost per kW and payback

A 13.2kW system costs more in total, but usually less per kW than a 10kW system. Whether payback is faster depends on self-consumption. If the extra 3.2kW mostly exports at 2c, the gain is small. If it charges an EV or a battery, the gain can be large. Ask your installer for a saving estimate at both sizes using your real usage data.

## A quick way to decide

1. Find your average daily usage on your bill.
2. Estimate how much happens during daylight.
3. Add planned loads: an EV adds about 7–10 kWh a day for typical commuting.
4. Check roof space with a site assessment.
5. Compare quotes at both sizes with the same panel and inverter models.
